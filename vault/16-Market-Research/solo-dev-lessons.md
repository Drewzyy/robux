# Solo dev lessons
What people who build alone report about staying finished, staying healthy, and shipping on a rhythm.

## Do this
- Cut the first version until it feels uncomfortably small; one loop, one map, one reward ladder. Guides warn a plan of 50 levels at one week each eats a year.
- Finish and publish the first game even if it flops; a 2022 r/gamedev author said finished versus abandoned came down to scope.
- Ship small updates every 5 to 14 days. One case study claims the fastest growers updated every 5 to 7 days (UNVERIFIED, labeled illustrative).
- Set work hours and rest days; DevForum and burnout guides agree on breaks. Our rule of thumb: stop new work on one day a week (UNVERIFIED).
- Keep a one-page "next three things" list; do not start a fourth until one ships.
- Write every update in a public log so players and you can see progress.

## Scope rules
- One sentence for the loop: "Do X to get Y so you can Z."
- No more than 3 systems at launch (loop, progression, one social feature). Rule of thumb, UNVERIFIED.
- Content that repeats (levels, items, maps) must be generated from data tables so one new row is a new item.
- Reuse your own assets across games; the Fantasy Forest author reused animals from an earlier game.
- Anything that needs another player online at launch (queues, trades) is a risk; build solo-playable first. A DevForum thread titled "Worried my multiplayer game won't have enough players at launch" shows the fear is common.
- Cut list: write what you will not build, and date it.

## Workflow that people report
- Core loop playable in a day or two, then polish; do not start with menus, lobbies or custom UI.
- Playtest with real players early; watch where they quit rather than asking opinions.
- Read analytics weekly: session length, return rate, where the funnel breaks.
- Version every update; keep the last stable version one click away.
- Keep tests on the first join and the purchase flow before each publish.
- Answer bug reports in one place (a group wall, a comments channel) so nothing is lost.

## Burnout signs and responses
- Signs: starting new projects instead of finishing, polishing forever, dread of opening Studio, ignoring players.
- Responses: shrink the next milestone to under a week, take a planned break, post a small win, ask for outside eyes.
- Isolation is a repeated theme; one guide points to solo-dev communities as an on-ramp.
- Do not read all comments daily; batch feedback once a week.

## Release cadence template
| Cadence | What ships | Where announced |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | One small content drop or fix | Game page update text, group wall |
| Every 2 to 4 weeks | A themed event or feature | Short video, Discord post |
| Every 3 months | A larger feature | Longer clip, update log |
Rule of thumb, UNVERIFIED. Skip a drop rather than ship a broken one.

## Weekly rhythm example (our suggestion)
- Monday: read analytics and bug reports, pick one fix and one feature.
- Tuesday to Thursday: build and playtest.
- Friday: publish if stable, write the update note.
- Weekend: rest, light community replies only.

## What the research did not settle
- Whether frequent updates themselves help ranking; one blog titled "Why Roblox Updates Kill Your Own Momentum" argues badly timed updates can hurt (opinion, UNVERIFIED).
- Whether to ship early or polish first; the same site says the algorithm decides, which is unproven.
- Best team size; Wonder Works grew to 90 and then cut to 12 (UNVERIFIED), a warning against hiring ahead of revenue.

## For our Roblox games
Copy: tiny scope, finished-first mindset, weekly cadence, data-driven content tables, one stable rollback. Never copy: another dev's assets, scripts or UI, or crunch habits. No paid tools or assets; use free Roblox Studio features only.
