# Curiosity and mystery

Curiosity appears when a player knows a little and wants to know more. Open a gap, then close it with a reward. See [[03-Game-Psychology/lessons-from-classic-games]].

## Do this
- Show something partly: a locked door, a glowing crack, a silhouette, a locked pet slot. Players want to see it fully.
- Place a teaser within 60 s of spawn and pay it off within 10 minutes.
- Use 3 layers of secrets: easy (most players find), medium (explorers), deep (community puzzle).
- Give small clues: sounds, footprints, floating dust, odd lighting. Each clue should point somewhere real.
- Reward discovery with something meaningful: a badge, a cosmetic, a shortcut, a lore page. See [[05-Progression/achievements-and-badges]].
- Keep a hint system for deep puzzles: after 10 minutes, a faint clue appears.
- Add rotating mysteries (weekly secret) so veterans have reasons to look again.
- Never hide required progress behind pure guessing.

## Why it works
- Closing a gap feels good; opening new ones keeps players moving.
- Secrets create word of mouth and community wikis for free.
- Exploration supports different play styles. See [[03-Game-Psychology/motivation-coverage-checklist]].

## How to build it in Roblox
- Hidden areas: use Attachments with beams, odd color accents, or sound zones to hint.
- Secrets tracked per player with a counter and a "secrets found 3/12" UI shown only after the first find.
- Clues can be diegetic: NPC lines, signs, or wall art that changes.
- Use Roblox badges for secrets (free to award) to give permanent recognition.
- Gate with a soft lock: a puzzle or a skill check, never an arbitrary key hunt.
- Time-based reveals: an event schedule that reveals a new corner each week.

## Numbers (starting points)
- First teaser within 60 s; first reveal within 10 min.
- 10-15 percent of players should find a deep secret within a month.
- One secret per 2-3 zones for casual players; more for explorer-focused games.
- Hint cooldown 10 min; clue radius 20-40 studs.

## Mistakes
- Secrets with no payoff.
- Mystery used to hide poor tutorials.
- Pixel-hunting or random-click puzzles on phones.
- Spoiling by showing hint UI too early.
- Overstuffing: when everything is mysterious, nothing is.

## Mystery pacing
1. Tease (door, sound).
2. Clue (a note, a rune).
3. Attempt (puzzle, skill).
4. Reveal (reward and a new question).
Repeat with a bigger question every chapter.

## Playtest check
- Ask testers what they think is behind the teaser; if they have no guess, it is not working.
- Count how many find the first secret without a hint.
- Watch for frustration sighs at deep puzzles and add the hint earlier.

## For our Roblox games
Copy the technique: gaps, layered secrets, diegetic clues, and fair hints. Never copy another game's specific riddles, lore, ciphers, or hidden-room layouts. Write original mysteries.
